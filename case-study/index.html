Overview

A CV says what you did. Your work shows how you think. MAKVO is built on that idea.

MAKVO is a home for designers, developers, studios and the founders who hire them. It brings together things that usually live in separate tools: a portfolio, a professional network, a project marketplace, video interviews and a shared workspace for the project itself.

Dsquare designed the product experience end to end and built a high-fidelity, fully clickable prototype to test the idea before any backend work. Every major flow works, from browsing a case study to hiring its author, interviewing them and running the project.

The challenge

Hiring is split across too many tools.

Creative professionals keep their best work on portfolio sites, their reputation on social networks and their contracts on freelance marketplaces. Clients judge talent from keyword-matched CVs, then move to email, a video app and a project tool to actually work together.

Each handoff loses context. A great case study rarely leads straight to a conversation, and a finished project rarely comes back as proof of what someone can do. Most marketplaces also look the same: dense dashboards of cards that treat people as listings.

The brief was to connect all of it into one calm, premium product that designers and developers would actually want to be seen on.

What we designed

Eighteen product areas, one connected flow.

Discovery that starts with the work

An editorial feed of finished projects, filterable by craft, technology, industry and style. Every project links to the people who made it, with a clear route to hire them.

Profiles people are proud to share

A portfolio-first profile with services and starting prices, Work DNA (style, industries, strengths), experience and verified reviews. Availability is always visible, and Mantis green always means open to work.

Opportunities with thoughtful applications

Briefs show budget, timeline, deliverables and references, plus people recommended for the project. Applying is a four-step flow: pick relevant work, explain your approach, set a price against the client's budget and preview what the client will see.

Conversations that turn into projects

Messaging sits next to the work. From any conversation you can share a project, invite someone to an interview, create a project workspace or hire, without leaving the thread. A docked chat follows you around the app.

A workspace that ends in proof

After hiring, the team gets tasks, files, meetings, milestones and payments in one place. When the project ships, the client confirms it and it becomes a Verified project on everyone's profile.

Dashboards for both sides

Creators see earnings, applications, profile views and their availability. Clients see applicants, shortlists, interviews, payments and talent recommendations. One switch moves between the two views.

Design system

Applying the Spring palette.

We applied MAKVO's brand identity across the whole product and turned it into a working design system. Each colour has one job, so meaning stays consistent everywhere: Spring for the primary action, Mantis for open to work, Picture Book Green for hired and success, and Nuit Blanche for links and information.

Responsive

Designed for every screen, not shrunk to fit.

The app runs in a browser with a persistent sidebar on desktop, an icon rail on laptops and tablets, and a bottom tab bar on phones. Layouts that split into two columns on a laptop stack cleanly on a tablet, and every screen was checked for overflow, legibility and touch targets.

Interviews in the flow

Schedule a call, run it in a video-style room with captions, chat and private notes, then rate the candidate and decide: hire, second interview, shortlist or reject.

Reduced-motion support, a minimum readable text size, perfectly round portraits, and themes that load without a flash.

What's next

From prototype to product.

The prototype was structured so real services can slot in without a redesign: a database and authentication, payments held in milestones, real-time messaging, video interviews, file storage and matching based on portfolio content.

The next phase is testing the core loop with designers, developers and studios, then building the backend behind the screens that work best.
